# OathPath Vision

## Our Vision

**OathPath exists to help people walk into their U.S. naturalization interview feeling prepared, capable, and confident because they have practiced the journey—not just memorized the answers.**

We are building more than a citizenship test-preparation app.

We are building an **AI companion for the path to naturalization**: a product that helps users learn the required material, remember it over time, practice speaking it aloud, become comfortable with the interview format, understand where they are improving, identify what still needs work, and steadily build evidence that they are ready.

Our aspiration is simple:

> **By the time a user walks into their naturalization interview, the experience should feel familiar.**

---

## Why OathPath Exists

Preparing for the U.S. naturalization process can be intimidating.

The official material tells applicants what they need to know, but knowing what to study is only part of the challenge. Users still have to:

- understand unfamiliar civics concepts;
- retain information over days and weeks;
- recall answers without seeing multiple-choice options;
- understand questions when spoken aloud;
- respond verbally in English when required;
- become comfortable interacting with an interviewer;
- practice reading and writing where applicable;
- understand which questions are difficult for them;
- know whether they are actually becoming ready;
- stay motivated through a preparation process that may last weeks or months.

Many people can study extensively and still wonder:

> **“Am I actually ready?”**

OathPath exists to answer that question with more than reassurance.

It should help users build **real evidence of readiness**.

---

## Why the Name OathPath

The name represents both the destination and the journey.

**Oath** represents the Oath of Allegiance—the meaningful conclusion of the naturalization process.

**Path** represents everything required to reach it: learning, remembering, practicing, improving, building confidence, and preparing for the real interview.

The name should remind the team of an important product principle:

> **The oath is the destination. The journey is the product.**

Users should not feel like they are simply completing a question bank.

They should feel like they are progressing along a path.

---

## Who We Are Building For

Our primary user is **a person preparing for the U.S. naturalization process, N-400 interview, and citizenship test who wants to become knowledgeable, comfortable, confident, and genuinely ready.**

Our users may:

- be early in the naturalization process;
- already have an interview scheduled;
- have studied before but still feel uncertain;
- speak English as a second language;
- understand written English better than spoken English;
- know the answer but struggle to say it confidently;
- feel nervous about the interview environment;
- prefer studying through text, voice, or a combination of both;
- have only a few minutes available on some days;
- want clear evidence that their preparation is working.

We should never assume that difficulty answering a question means a user is incapable.

A user may not know the answer yet. They may have forgotten it. They may not have understood the spoken question. They may know the concept but struggle to express it in English. Speech recognition may have misunderstood them. Or they may simply be nervous.

OathPath should help distinguish these situations and respond intelligently.

---

## The Experience We Want to Create

OathPath should feel like a **persistent, intelligent coach that understands the user's preparation journey**.

The product should continually answer three questions:

> **Where am I?**  
> **What should I do next?**  
> **Am I becoming more ready?**

The user should never have to decide from a wall of study options what to do next.

OathPath should recommend the next best activity based on what it knows about the user's preparation.

That might be:

- learning new civics material;
- reviewing questions that are beginning to fade;
- practicing difficult answers aloud;
- completing a short reading exercise;
- retrying recently missed questions;
- doing a five-minute practice session;
- taking a realistic mock interview;
- maintaining readiness before an upcoming interview.

The experience should evolve as the user evolves.

---

## The Journey to Readiness

Readiness is not achieved in one session.

OathPath should deliberately guide users through a multi-stage journey:

**Uncertain → Oriented → Learning → Remembering → Speaking → Practicing → Performing → Ready**

These stages are not decorative labels. They should influence:

- recommendations;
- home-screen content;
- AI coaching behavior;
- practice difficulty;
- notification strategy;
- readiness calculations;
- milestones;
- gamification;
- interview simulation;
- progress messaging.

The product must never create the impression that a user is ready simply because they completed a handful of questions correctly.

Readiness must be earned through repeated evidence over time.

---

## Readiness Is Our Core Product Outcome

The primary question OathPath should help answer is not:

> “How many questions have I completed?”

It is:

> **“How ready am I, and what evidence supports that?”**

OathPath should maintain an explainable Readiness model based on signals such as:

- breadth of official material covered;
- active recall without hints;
- repeated accuracy across multiple days;
- retention over time;
- performance on previously weak questions;
- spoken-answer practice;
- realistic mock interview performance;
- reading and writing practice where applicable;
- recent consistency;
- confidence calibration;
- strength and diversity of the evidence collected.

Readiness should never be presented as an official prediction of USCIS outcomes.

It is an OathPath assessment of preparation evidence.

The product should always explain what is affecting the score and what action is most likely to improve it.

---

## AI Everywhere, Chatbot Nowhere

AI is central to OathPath, but the product should not feel like a generic chatbot wrapped around citizenship questions.

The principle is:

> **AI should be embedded throughout the experience rather than confined to a chat screen.**

The AI companion should be able to act as:

### Teacher
Explain unfamiliar concepts clearly and at the user's level.

### Study Coach
Recommend what to practice next and why.

### Practice Partner
Ask questions naturally through voice or text.

### Encourager
Recognize meaningful progress and help users recover from setbacks.

### Evaluator
Determine whether a response meaningfully matches an authoritative accepted answer.

### Interview Simulator
Conduct realistic, neutral mock naturalization interviews.

### Progress Guide
Explain readiness, weak areas, trends, and the next milestone.

These roles may be implemented through different AI agents and product services, but they should feel like one coherent companion to the user.

---

## Voice and Text Are Equal Citizens

OathPath must support **voice, text, and seamless movement between the two**.

The naturalization interview is fundamentally conversational. Users need more than recognition-based learning.

They need to hear questions and practice producing answers.

Voice practice should therefore be a first-class part of OathPath.

The experience should allow users to:

- hear questions aloud;
- answer verbally;
- type instead when voice is inconvenient;
- switch between voice and text without losing progress;
- interrupt naturally during realtime conversations;
- retry when the system may have misheard them;
- practice without being unfairly penalized for accent or speech-recognition errors.

The user should feel like they are speaking with a patient human coach, not operating a voice command interface.

---

## The AI Personality

The OathPath companion should feel:

**Warm, but not sugary.**  
**Encouraging, but not dishonest.**  
**Confident, but never overconfident.**  
**Patient with repetition.**  
**Culturally respectful.**  
**Concise unless more explanation is useful.**  
**Comfortable admitting uncertainty.**  
**Never condescending about English ability.**

Encouragement should be specific and earned.

Good:

> “You remembered this correctly three times this week. That answer is becoming reliable.”

Better than:

> “Amazing! You're doing great!”

When a user struggles, the AI should help them understand what happened and what to do next.

When a user succeeds, the AI should connect the success to meaningful progress.

### The Voice Is Chosen by the Learner

The traits above are the **default** companion, and they are the **floor**
beneath every other one.

But a coach who works for one person does not work for everyone. Some people
are steadied by warmth. Others are bored by it and would rather have a coach
who is funny, or blunt, or willing to roast a bad answer out loud. Preparing
for this interview takes months, and a companion nobody enjoys is a companion
nobody comes back to.

So OathPath lets the learner choose how their coach speaks to them — academic,
supportive, playful, or unfiltered.

**Being fun is a legitimate way to keep someone practising.** It is not a
compromise of the mission; for some learners it is how the mission gets done.
The companion can be loving and patient, and it can be sharp and irreverent.
People differ, and the product should let them say which one they want.

**This is a choice the learner makes, never one the product makes for them.**
The default is the warm, supportive voice described above. It changes only
when somebody asks it to, and it changes back in one tap.

And no chosen style crosses the floor:

- Never about the learner's English, accent, grammar, or pronunciation.
- Never about their country of origin, immigration status, religion, race, or family.
- Never implying they will fail, or will not become a citizen.
- Never changing the verdict, the accepted answer, or the readiness score.
- **The joke is about the answer. It is never about the person.**

That distinction is what separates this from the entries on the "What We Will
Not Build" list below. Those describe things done *to* a learner who never
asked for them. This is a learner choosing their own coach.

---

## OathPath Owns the Truth

AI should never be the authoritative source of official naturalization facts.

Our foundational rule is:

> **OathPath owns the truth. AI owns the interaction.**

Official USCIS questions, accepted answers, test versions, current-official answers, and other authoritative content must come from verified and versioned OathPath data.

AI may use that information to:

- teach;
- explain;
- personalize;
- converse;
- grade semantically;
- simulate;
- motivate;
- recommend.

But AI must not invent official questions, rules, or answers from model memory when verified data is available.

Accuracy and trust take precedence over creativity.

---

## Confidence Must Be Built, Not Manufactured

One of the most important outcomes of OathPath is confidence.

But confidence should emerge from evidence.

Users should become confident because they have:

- practiced repeatedly;
- retained material over time;
- answered questions aloud;
- recovered from mistakes;
- strengthened weak areas;
- experienced realistic interview simulations;
- seen consistent improvement.

OathPath should never tell a user to feel confident simply because confidence is emotionally desirable.

The goal is to create the conditions that make confidence rational.

---

## Learning Should Be Active

OathPath should favor **retrieval, repetition, spacing, and realistic practice** over passive exposure.

Users should increasingly be asked to produce answers rather than merely recognize them.

Our learning progression should move from:

**See it → Understand it → Recall it → Say it → Retain it → Perform it**

Study should adapt to the learner.

The system should spend more time on what is weak, revisit what is becoming stale, and continue sampling what appears strong so that mastery is verified rather than assumed.

---

## The Daily Experience

Every session should have a clear purpose.

The ideal daily loop is:

**Open OathPath**  
↓  
**Understand current status**  
↓  
**Receive a recommended next action**  
↓  
**Complete a manageable practice session**  
↓  
**Receive immediate useful feedback**  
↓  
**See the impact on progress and readiness**  
↓  
**Understand what comes next**  
↓  
**Leave feeling more capable**

A user should be able to make meaningful progress in five minutes.

Longer sessions should be available, but they should never be required for the product to feel useful.

---

## Motivation and Engagement

Preparation takes consistency.

OathPath should help users build a sustainable practice habit through:

- daily goals;
- meaningful streaks;
- streak protection;
- small achievable sessions;
- progress milestones;
- practice points;
- achievements;
- weekly challenges;
- personalized reminders;
- readiness movement;
- session-end celebrations;
- intelligent re-engagement.

But gamification must support the mission.

We will not confuse engagement with readiness.

A streak shows consistency.

Points show activity.

Achievements mark milestones.

**None of these make someone ready.**

Readiness is based on learning evidence.

We should never create pressure, shame, fear, or unhealthy compulsion to increase engagement metrics.

---

## Notifications Should Feel Intelligent

Notifications should feel like messages from a coach who understands the user's journey, not from an app demanding attention.

Useful:

> “Five minutes is enough today. You have four review questions ready.”

> “Your government questions are improving. Try five out loud?”

> “Welcome back. No catching up required—I made you a short restart.”

Not useful:

> “You haven't studied today!!!”

The goal is to help users return, not make them feel guilty.

---

## Mobile First, Everywhere Capable

OathPath is primarily a mobile experience.

The phone should be the best place to:

- complete daily practice;
- talk with the AI coach;
- review questions;
- take quick quizzes;
- track progress;
- receive reminders;
- practice while life is happening.

Tablet and desktop must still feel intentional and polished.

Larger screens should improve information density, progress exploration, and long-form study without simply stretching a mobile interface.

---

## Trust Is a Feature

Users are preparing for a consequential government process.

Trust must be visible throughout the product.

OathPath should always make clear:

- OathPath is not USCIS;
- practice results are not official determinations;
- readiness is an OathPath preparation assessment;
- official facts are sourced from authoritative materials;
- dynamic answers are verified and time-stamped;
- users control their data and notification preferences;
- sensitive information is handled conservatively;
- AI may make mistakes and should be able to say when it is uncertain.

Trust is not legal copy buried in settings.

It is part of the user experience.

---

## What We Will Not Build

OathPath is not:

- a generic AI chatbot about immigration;
- a legal-advice service;
- a replacement for USCIS;
- a multiple-choice trivia game;
- a product that rewards endless screen time;
- a leaderboard-driven competition between applicants;
- a system that tells users they will pass;
- a system that sacrifices accuracy for engagement;
- a product that shames users for missing study sessions;
- a product that treats non-native English speakers as less capable.

These boundaries are part of the vision.

---

## Product Principles

Every major product decision should be tested against these principles.

### 1. The Journey Is the Product
We are helping users move toward readiness over time.

### 2. Always Answer “What Should I Do Next?”
The user should never be left without a clear next action.

### 3. AI Everywhere, Chatbot Nowhere
AI should improve the entire experience.

### 4. OathPath Owns the Truth
Authoritative data determines official facts.

### 5. Readiness Must Be Earned
Never manufacture confidence from weak evidence.

### 6. Voice Matters
Users must become comfortable answering aloud.

### 7. Practice Should Become More Realistic
Coaching decreases as the user approaches authentic interview simulation.

### 8. Encourage Without Manipulating
Motivation should support the user, not exploit them.

### 9. Respect the User
Never patronize, shame, or underestimate the learner.

### 10. Make Progress Visible
Users should understand how today's work connects to the larger journey.

### 11. Trust Before Delight
A beautiful wrong answer is still wrong.

### 12. Five Minutes Should Matter
Small sessions should create meaningful progress.

---

## What Success Looks Like

OathPath succeeds when users consistently report:

> **“I knew what to expect.”**

> **“I had practiced this before.”**

> **“I felt more comfortable answering out loud.”**

> **“I knew what I needed to work on.”**

> **“I could see myself getting better.”**

> **“The interview felt familiar.”**

Our success metrics should ultimately reflect these outcomes—not merely downloads, sessions, or time spent.

We should care about:

- repeated practice over time;
- growth in verified mastery;
- improvement in spoken performance;
- reduction in weak areas;
- consistent realistic mock performance;
- progression in readiness evidence;
- sustained engagement without unhealthy dependency;
- user-reported confidence;
- user-reported interview preparedness.

---

## The North Star

The best version of OathPath feels less like software and more like having a knowledgeable, patient coach beside you throughout the naturalization journey.

A coach who knows what you have studied.

A coach who remembers what you struggle with.

A coach who challenges you when you are ready.

A coach who explains when you are confused.

A coach who lets you practice without embarrassment.

A coach who tells you the truth about your preparation.

A coach who helps turn uncertainty into evidence, evidence into confidence, and confidence into readiness.

That is the product we are building.

> **OathPath helps people travel the path from uncertainty to readiness—and ultimately toward the oath they have worked to reach.**
